### Detailed Proof and Analysis

#### Understanding the Problem

We need to compute the product of two sums:
1. The first sum is $\sum_{k=1}^{20} \log_{5^k} 3^{k^2}$.
2. The second sum is $\sum_{k=1}^{100} \log_{9^k} 25^k$.

The result should be $21,000$.

#### Simplifying $\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2}$

Recall that $\log_b a = \frac{\ln a}{\ln b}$. Also, the logarithm power rule is $\log_b a^c = c \log_b a$.

First, simplify $\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2}$:
\[
\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2} = \frac{\ln 3^{k^2}}{\ln 5^k} = \frac{k^2 \ln 3}{k \ln 5} = k \cdot \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 5} = k \log_5 3.
\]
Therefore, the first sum becomes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{20} \log_{5^k} 3^{k^2} = \sum_{k=1}^{20} k \log_5 3 = \log_5 3 \sum_{k=1}^{20} k = \log_5 3 \cdot \frac{20 \cdot 21}{2} = 210 \log_5 3.
\]

#### Simplifying $\log_{9^k} 25^k$

Now, simplify $\log_{9^k} 25^k$:
\[
\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{\ln 25^k}{\ln 9^k} = \frac{k \ln 25}{k \ln 9} = \frac{\ln 25}{\ln 9}.
\]
Notice that $\ln 25 = 2 \ln 5$ and $\ln 9 = 2 \ln 3$, so:
\[
\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{2 \ln 5}{2 \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3} = \log_3 5.
\]
Thus, the second sum becomes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{100} \log_{9^k} 25^k = \sum_{k=1}^{100} \log_3 5 = 100 \log_3 5.
\]

#### Calculating the Product

Now, multiply the two sums:
\[
(210 \log_5 3) \cdot (100 \log_3 5) = 21000 \cdot (\log_5 3 \cdot \log_3 5).
\]
But $\log_5 3 \cdot \log_3 5 = 1$ because:
\[
\log_b a \cdot \log_a b = \frac{\ln a}{\ln b} \cdot \frac{\ln b}{\ln a} = 1.
\]
Therefore, the product is $21000 \cdot 1 = 21000$.

#### Formal Proof in Lean

In Lean, `Real.logb b x` is defined as $\log_b x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b}$. We need to compute each sum carefully using Lean's definitions and properties of logarithms.

1. For the first sum, we need to prove that for each $k$, $\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2} = k \log_5 3$. This follows from logarithm properties as shown above.
   - Note that in Lean, $3^{k^2}$ is `3 ^ (k ^ 2)` and $5^k$ is `5 ^ k`.
   - We'll use `Real.logb_pow` and `Real.logb_rpow` to manipulate the exponents.

2. For the second sum, we need to prove that $\log_{9^k} 25^k = \log_3 5$.
   - Here, $9^k = (3^2)^k = 3^{2k}$ and $25^k = (5^2)^k = 5^{2k}$.
   - So $\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{\ln 25^k}{\ln 9^k} = \frac{2k \ln 5}{2k \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3} = \log_3 5$.

However, in Lean, `9 ^ k` is not automatically simplified to `3 ^ (2 * k)`, so we might need to do this manually or find another approach. 

But perhaps it's easier to note that $\ln 25^k = k \ln 25 = 2k \ln 5$ and $\ln 9^k = k \ln 9 = 2k \ln 3$, so:
\[
\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{2k \ln 5}{2k \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}.
\]
Thus, we don't need to rewrite $9^k$ as $3^{2k}$ explicitly.

#### Formal Steps

1. **First Sum Simplification**:
   - For each $k$, $\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2} = \frac{\ln 3^{k^2}}{\ln 5^k} = \frac{k^2 \ln 3}{k \ln 5} = k \cdot \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 5}$.
   - The sum $\sum_{k=1}^{20} k = 210$, so the first sum is $210 \cdot \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 5}$.

2. **Second Sum Simplification**:
   - For each $k$, $\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{\ln 25^k}{\ln 9^k} = \frac{k \ln 25}{k \ln 9} = \frac{\ln 25}{\ln 9}$.
   - But $\ln 25 = 2 \ln 5$ and $\ln 9 = 2 \ln 3$, so $\log_{9^k} 25^k = \frac{2 \ln 5}{2 \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}$.
   - The sum $\sum_{k=1}^{100} \log_{9^k} 25^k = 100 \cdot \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}$.

3. **Product Calculation**:
   - The product is $210 \cdot \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 5} \cdot 100 \cdot \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3} = 210 \cdot 100 = 21000$.

#### Challenges in Lean

1. **Logarithm Simplifications**: We need to carefully use Lean's `Real.log_pow`, `Real.log_mul`, etc., to simplify expressions like $\ln 3^{k^2}$ and $\ln 5^k$.
2. **Sum Calculations**: The sum $\sum_{k=1}^n k = n(n+1)/2$ is straightforward, but we need to ensure Lean can compute it for specific cases like $n=20$.
3. **Numerical Constants**: Lean will require exact computations for $\sum_{k=1}^{20} k$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{100} 1$.

#### Step-by-Step Abstract Plan

   - Prove that $\log_{5^k} 3^{k^2} = k \cdot \log_5 3$ for each $k \in \mathbb{N}^+$.
   - Deduce that the first sum is $210 \cdot \log_5 3$.

2. **Simplify the Second Summand**:
   - Prove that $\log_{9^k} 25^k = \log_3 5$ for each $k \in \mathbb{N}^+$.

3. **Multiply the Sums**:
   - Compute $(210 \cdot \log_5 3) \cdot (100 \cdot \log_3 5) = 21000$ using $\log_5 3 \cdot \log_3 5 = 1$.
